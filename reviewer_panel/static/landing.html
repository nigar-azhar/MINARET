MINARET

Urdu and English Islamic lectures, transcribed, verified and connected.

Every quotation, traced to its source.

Lectures move between Urdu or English and the Arabic of the Quran, hadith and duas. Speech recognition gets that Arabic wrong. MINARET corrects the transcript, checks every quotation against its canonical source, has a reviewer confirm it, and turns the result into a knowledge graph you can query and a question-answering system that cites the lecture.

The problem

One lecture, three languages at once

A speaker explains in Urdu or English and quotes the Arabic of the Quran from memory. Speech recognition hears the explanation well and the Arabic badly: missing vowel marks, misheard letters, words run together. A quotation that is wrong is worse than none.

How it works

Follow one segment through the pipeline

Ten seconds of a real lecture from the released data, through every stage.

  1. 1

    Listen

    A recording from the publisher. MINARET never needs to store the audio: it reads it from the publisher's link.

  2. 2

    Transcribe V0

    faster-whisper large-v3-turbo produces timed segments.

  3. 3

    Correct V1

    A language model corrects each segment on its own, keeping its timestamps. It fixes spelling and punctuation and restores Arabic, but it can also get things wrong.

  4. 4

    Find and verify E1 · V2

    Ayahs, hadith, duas and topics are extracted. Each Quranic and dua quotation is scored against the full canonical corpus; only a confident match replaces the text.

  5. 5

    Review V3 · E2

    A reviewer checks every segment and entity against the audio. Anything unclear is flagged and goes to a super reviewer, who makes the final decision.

  6. 6

    Connect

    The reviewed lecture becomes a knowledge graph: every quotation links to its verse, and every verse to the Quran Ontology and SemanticTafsir; hadith link to SemanticHadith.

  7. 7

    Ask

    Questions in Urdu or English are answered from the lecture itself, with the passage cited, and refused when the lecture does not say.

Try it

Run the review yourself

The reviewer panel is a local simulator of the review stage: load a lecture's V2 and E1, review it, and produce V3 and E2 in the pipeline's format. It can also run every automatic stage and answer questions over the graph and the lectures.

Review a lecture

Segments in order, entities beside them, flags and super review. Replay a real review to see how the released V3 was reached.

Run the pipeline

Transcribe, correct, extract and validate, then build the knowledge graph and the RAG index, one stage at a time.

Ask a question

RAG questions over the lectures, and the paper's 16 competency questions over the knowledge graph.

Plug in your models

Point each stage at LM Studio, Ollama, OpenRouter or any OpenAI-compatible server, with your own keys.
